In one sentence

This is a guide, with ready-made templates, to put you or your company on Wikidata (Wikipedia's open database, which search engines and AIs read) and, when there are enough sources, on Wikipedia. It is for business owners, independent professionals, content creators and anyone who looks after another person's image. It teaches the path Wikipedia itself accepts: gather articles published by others, declare that the subject is you, and ask an independent editor to review. All you need is your old articles and an AI assistant, like Claude Code or Codex (programs that search the internet and write the texts for you), to do the heavy work.

How it works, no jargon

Five steps, in the order Wikipedia accepts

Think of a notary's office: first the documents, then the record, and finally the request for an outsider to stamp it.

1

Gather the proof AI + you

Like collecting documents for the notary: newspaper articles, interviews, awards. The AI searches and opens each page; you remember where things came out and send what you have.

2

Create the account with your name you

Like signing a document: a person's name, not a brand. The password and the CAPTCHA (the "I'm not a robot" test) are always yours.

3

Declare the connection AI writes, you approve

Like saying "I'm an interested party" before speaking in a meeting. It is the account's first edit.

4

Fill in the record on Wikidata AI

Like a registration form where every field has its receipt stapled on: "founded the company", with the link to the article that says so.

5

Draft and review request AI writes, you send

The article stays on your own draft page. An independent editor reads it, adjusts it and decides whether it goes live.

The precautions that keep the article from being deleted

🚫 Don't write like an ad

No "renowned", "leader", "benchmark". Just facts, each with a source.

🚫 Don't use a brand name on the account

An account with a company name is rejected. It happened in the real case and was fixed.

🚫 Don't ask friends to create it

Several accounts, or friends who don't declare the connection, get detected and can all be blocked.

🚫 Don't publish directly

With a connection, the text goes to a draft and an outsider reviews it.

🚫 Don't use a paid source as proof

An article marked "advertorial" only confirms simple facts, like a date.

🚫 Don't fight over the text later

Once published, the article is not yours. Ask for changes on the article's "Talk" tab.

What this guide does not promise

It does not guarantee an article on Wikipedia: the editor community decides. It guarantees that you go in the right way, with the best you have, and that you know honestly what is still missing.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need to know how to program?

No. The AI does the research and writes. You read, approve, enter the password and solve the CAPTCHAs.

Can I add my new company?

On Wikidata, maybe, if there is a public source. On Wikipedia, only when independent newspapers or magazines have already written about it. The guide tells you this honestly.

How long does it take?

Wikidata: a few hours, if the articles exist. A Wikipedia article: depends on the editors' review: weeks to months, and it may not be approved.

What if I already have an article?

Don't edit the article about you. Suggest changes on the "Talk" tab at the top of it, with a source, and declare the connection.

Can I pay someone to do it?

Paid editing has its own rules at Wikimedia and must be declared. Doing it yourself, with the connection declared, is simpler and more transparent.

Does this really show up on Google and in AIs?

Wikidata and Wikipedia are open and widely used by search engines and by those who train AIs. There is no guarantee of when or how each service will use them. The sure gain is having a neutral, sourced record linked to your website.
